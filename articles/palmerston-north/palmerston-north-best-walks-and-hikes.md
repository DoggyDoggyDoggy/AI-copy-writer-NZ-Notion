# 6 Best Hikes & Scenic Walks in Palmerston North: Gorge Trails & Tararua Ridges

Set on the fertile Manawatū Plains beneath the dramatic wind-carved spine of the Tararua and Ruahine mountain ranges, Palmerston North (Te Papa-i-Oea) is an underrated gateway for authentic North Island wilderness tramping.

Here, the mighty Manawatū River carves through ancient geological faults, creating deep forested river gorges, crystalline rocky swimming streams, and wild ridgelines dominated by giant spinning wind turbines. From hiking beneath ancient tawa and podocarp trees to marvel at the 6-metre Māori warrior sculpture of Whatonga, to exploring rugged platinum-prospecting gorge tracks, here is the local tramper’s guide to the six best walks and hikes across Palmerston North and the Manawatū.

### 1. Manawatū Gorge Tawa Loop Walk (Te Āpiti)
- **Distance & Duration**: 4.2 km loop | 1.5–2 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +190 m | Moderate (well-graded gravel track with stepped switchbacks)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Ashhurst / Western car park (State Highway 3, 15 mins east of Palmerston North).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (high-value native bird sanctuary). Clean toilets and visitor interpretation panels at base.

The flagship native bush walk of the Manawatū region. Starting over a picturesque wooden footbridge, the trail ascends through pristine native podocarp-hardwood rainforest dense with ancient tawa, rimu, nikau palms, and giant silver ferns. Midway along the high ridge, you will encounter the awe-inspiring **Whatonga sculpture**—a majestic 6.2-metre steel and timber sculpture depicting the great Māori ancestral navigator of the Kurahaupō waka. The lookouts provide sheer views straight down into the turbulent, emerald-green waters of the Manawatū Gorge.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Take the short 5-minute spur track to the Gorge Lookout just past Whatonga. Keep an ear out for the bell-like calls of the bellbird (*korimako*) and the curious fluttering of fantails (*pīwakawaka*).

### 2. The Sledge Track (Kahuterawa Valley)
- **Distance & Duration**: 5.0 to 11.0 km return (options to Quartz Creek or Platinum Mines) | 2–4.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +340 m | Strenuous (rocky gorge scrambling, swing bridges, uneven bush tracks)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: End of Kahuterawa Road car park (off Old West Road, 20 mins south of Palmerston North).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs allowed on leash. Toilets and picnic area at road end.

A rugged, adventurous tramp that feels a world away from civilization. The Sledge Track follows the boulder-strewn Kahuterawa Stream deep into the foothills of the Tararua Ranges. Hand-built by local outdoor legend Ian Argyle, the trail winds past deep natural river swimming holes, cascading waterfalls, and narrow rock gorges spanned by steel swing bridges. More adventurous trampers can push up the steep switchbacks to the historic **Platinum Mines** and **Argyle Rocks** lookouts.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Wear sturdy boots with good ankle support. The rocky gorge path stays damp and can be slippery. In summer, bring a towel—the natural swimming pool at **Quartz Creek junction** is one of the best freshwater swim spots in the lower North Island.

### 3. Victoria Esplanade & Manawatū River Pathway
- **Distance & Duration**: 4.0 to 10.0 km (customizable paved paths) | 1–2 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Easy (fully sealed, accessible multi-use path)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Victoria Esplanade main car park (Manawaroa Street, Palmerston North).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dog-friendly (designated off-leash exercise zones). Historic rose gardens, miniature train, playgrounds, and cafes.

Palmerston North’s premier urban green space. The Manawatū River Pathway traces the banks of the river for over 10 kilometres, passing beneath majestic native kōwhai and exotic deciduous trees. The pathway links seamlessly with the **Victoria Esplanade**, home to the world-renowned Dugald MacKenzie Rose Gardens (over 5,000 rose bushes blooming from November to April) and the Wildbase Recovery Centre for native wildlife.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Stop at the **Esplanade Cafe** for a morning flat white and fresh scone, then stroll through the Peter Black Conservatory to admire tropical indoor plants and orchids.

### 4. Te Āpiti Wind Farm Lookout & Ridge Walk
- **Distance & Duration**: 2.5 km return | 45 minutes to 1 hour
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +80 m | Easy (graded farm paths)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Te Āpiti Wind Farm viewing area (Saddle Road, 20 mins from Palmerston North).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (active sheep and cattle farmland). Public viewing shelter.

Stand directly beneath the colossal 70-metre-tall towers and 35-metre spinning blades of New Zealand’s first major wind farm. Located on the crest of the Ruahine Range where Cook Strait gales funnel through the Manawatū gap, the trail offers an exhilarating, windswept walk across open tussock pastures. The panoramic viewpoint takes in the entire sweep of the Manawatū Plains, Mount Bruce, and Mount Taranaki on crystal-clear days.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Hold onto your hat and jacket! The wind gusts up here can be ferocious. Bring a heavy windproof layer regardless of how calm it feels down in the city.

### 5. Te Ara o Mahurangi & Turitea Valley Bush Walk
- **Distance & Duration**: 3.5 km loop | 1 hour
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +110 m | Easy to Moderate (shaded bush pathways and stairs)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Old West Road / Turitea Road entrance (near Massey University campus).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted.

A tranquil, heavily wooded valley path that follows the Turitea Stream through native regenerating podocarp-broadleaf bush. Because it connects directly with the extensive forested conservation catchment of the Tararua foothills, the valley is a vital ecological corridor teeming with native birdlife, including kākā, tūī, grey warblers (*riroriro*), and kererū.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Combine this walk with a stroll through the adjacent heritage grounds and tree collections of **Massey University campus**.

---

### 6. Arapuke Forest Park Trails
- **Distance & Duration**: 4.0 to 8.0 km (multiple interlinked loops) | 1.5–2.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +260 m | Moderate (forestry tracks and native bush ridge paths)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Arapuke Forest Park car park (Kahuterawa Road).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs allowed on leash. Extensive mountain bike trail network with dedicated pedestrian walking tracks.

Set in the rugged hill country south of the city, Arapuke Forest Park spans hundreds of hectares of mature commercial forestry, regenerating native bush, and native wetland gullies. While it is celebrated as the Manawatū's premier mountain bike destination, its dedicated walking and trail running tracks climb to high ridge lookouts with sweeping vistas over the Tararua Ranges.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Be mindful of mountain bike crossing points. Keep to the dedicated uphill walking trails (marked with pedestrian signage) and listen for descending cyclists on shared fire roads.

## 🍃 Manawatū Tramping Guidelines & River Safety

1. **Gorge River Currents**: The Manawatū River through the gorge has deep vortexes and dangerous hydraulic currents. Do not attempt to swim in the main gorge channel. For safe swimming, use shallow pools on the Sledge Track or designated river beaches near Ashhurst Domain.
2. **Weather Changes in the Ranges**: The Tararua and Ruahine ranges generate sudden cloud inversions and heavy rain. Always carry a waterproof jacket when exploring the Sledge Track or Arapuke.
3. **Respect Farmland Boundaries**: When visiting the Te Āpiti Wind Farm track, stay strictly to marked paths and respect grazing livestock.

## Before You Hit the Trail: Palmerston North Trail Answers

**Is the full Manawatū Gorge track open?**
The historic highway through the bottom of the gorge is permanently closed due to massive slips, but the **Manawatū Gorge Tawa Loop Walk** (and the Te Araroa high-level walking track) at the western entrance is fully open and maintained by DOC.

**Are these tracks suitable for trail running?**
Yes! Palmerston North has an exceptionally active trail running scene. The Manawatū River Pathway is perfect for flat tempo runs, while the Sledge Track and Arapuke Forest offer technical climbs and elevation training.

**Can I visit the Whatonga sculpture without doing the full loop?**
Yes. You can walk directly up to Whatonga from the Ashhurst car park in about 30 to 40 minutes via the main track and return the same way if you want to shorten the walk.
