# 7 Best Hikes & Scenic Walks in Rotorua: Redwood Forests, Volcanic Lakes & Geothermal Trails

Rotorua is a landscape where the earth breathes. Set inside a massive volcanic caldera in the central North Island, the region is defined by rising plumes of geothermal steam, eighteen crystalline crater lakes, and dense native podocarp forests crowned by towering 70-metre-tall Californian coastal redwoods.

While many tourists stick to paid commercial geothermal parks, some of Rotorua’s most jaw-dropping volcanic terrain and serene forest sanctuaries can be explored completely on foot for free. From soaking in natural lakefront thermal hot springs at the end of a backcountry bush trail to running beneath a canopy of 120-year-old giant redwoods, here is the local tramper’s guide to the seven best walks and hikes in Rotorua.

### 1. Whakarewarewa Forest: Quarry & Pohaturoa Track Loop
- **Distance & Duration**: 7.5 km loop | 2–2.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +230 m | Moderate (well-maintained forest singletrack with stair climbs)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Redwoods Visitor Centre car park (Long Mile Road, Whakarewarewa).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted on selected walking tracks. Visitor centre, cafe, bike wash, and clean toilets at trailhead.

The Redwoods (Whakarewarewa Forest) is sacred ground for outdoor lovers. While casual walkers take the flat 30-minute Memorial Grove path beneath the giant 70-metre *Sequoia sempervirens* planted in 1901, trampers should head for the **Pohaturoa Track** (marked in yellow). The trail climbs past ancient fern trees through towering redwood groves to the historic Pohaturoa lookout. At the summit clearing, you get an astonishing panoramic vista of Rotorua city, Lake Rotorua, Mount Ngongotahā, and the active steam plumes rising from the Whakarewarewa Geothermal Valley and Pōhutu Geyser.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Start your walk before 8:30 AM. Morning light filtering through the misty redwood canopy creates ethereal sunbeams (*komaru*) that make for world-class photography.

### 2. Lake Tikitapu (Blue Lake) Circuit Track
- **Distance & Duration**: 5.5 km loop | 1.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +80 m | Easy (gently undulating track over pumice and forest paths)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Lake Tikitapu main beach car park (Tikitapu Drive, Tarawera Road).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted on track; designated off-leash dog exercise beach at northern end. Public toilets and gas BBQs at main beach.

Lake Tikitapu is a volcanic crater lake celebrated for its striking aquamarine colour, caused by the lake's pumice and rhyolite bottom reflecting clear sky light. The circuit track hugs the shoreline, transitioning between sheltered native bush (rimu, tawa, and tree ferns) and open ridgelines overlooking adjacent Lake Rotokakahi (the sacred Green Lake / *Tapu*). 

> **Local Trail Tip**: Pack swimwear and a towel. The clear, fresh waters of the main beach are the most popular summer swimming spot for locals in Rotorua.

### 3. Tarawera Trail (Te Wairoa to Hot Water Beach / Te Rātā Bay)
- **Distance & Duration**: 15.0 km one-way | 4.5–6 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +410 m cumulative | Moderate to Strenuous (undulating lake bluffs, bush stairs)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Tarawera Trail car park (Tarawera Road, opposite Te Wairoa Buried Village).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (private Māori tribal land & conservation estate). Toilets at trailhead, Kotukutuku Bay, and campsite.

A bucket-list North Island day hike combining volcanic drama, Māori cultural heritage, and natural luxury. Starting near the historic Buried Village (devastated by the 1886 Mount Tarawera eruption), the trail traverses the indigenous forest along the southern shoreline of Lake Tarawera. You'll cross footbridges over crystalline streams and climb exposed bluffs with dramatic views of Mount Tarawera's split summit. The ultimate reward awaits at **Te Rātā Bay (Hot Water Beach)**, where natural geothermal springs bubble up directly through the lakebed gravel.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Dig your own personal hot pool in the shoreline sand where the boiling thermal water mixes with the cool lake. **Book the Totally Tarawera water taxi in advance** to take you back across the lake to the trailhead instead of walking 15 km back.

### 4. Lake Ōkāreka Boardwalk & Boyes Beach Walk
- **Distance & Duration**: 5.5 km return | 1.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Easy (fully accessible wooden boardwalk and gravel path)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Acacia Road car park or Boyes Beach (Lake Ōkāreka).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs allowed on leash. Bird hide and public restrooms along the route.

If you are looking for a gentle, scenic stroll with world-class native birdwatching, Lake Ōkāreka is unbeatable. The trail features a lengthy, beautifully engineered wooden boardwalk that floats directly over protected wetland reed beds. You will encounter dabchicks (weweia), purple swamphens (pūkeko), New Zealand scaup (pāpango), and black swans nesting in the shallows. The walk finishes at a secluded sandy cove sheltered by native pōhutukawa trees.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Stop at the covered wooden bird-watching hide along the boardwalk. Bring binoculars to spot breeding pairs of rare dabchicks without disturbing them.

### 5. Mount Ngongotahā Nature Loop & Jubilee Track
- **Distance & Duration**: 4.8 km return (Jubilee Track) or 2.5 km Nature Loop | 1.5–2 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +320 m | Moderate (steady bush climb over roots and steps)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Violet Bonnington Reserve car park (junction of Paradise Valley Road and Clayton Road).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs strictly prohibited. No toilets on track.

Mount Ngongotahā is a 757-metre rhyolite volcanic dome rising directly behind Rotorua. Unlike the commercial forestry areas, the eastern flank of the mountain protects a rare pocket of unlogged, virgin native podocarp-broadleaf rainforest. Climbing beneath towering ancient rimu, rātā, and tawa trees, you’ll be surrounded by thick carpets of crown ferns and moss. The trail ascends steadily to the summit communication tower, passing through cool, damp rainforest microclimates.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Listen for the unmistakable bell-like notes of the tūī and korimako. The unlogged podocarp canopy provides abundant seasonal berries, making this one of Rotorua’s richest native bird corridors.

### 6. Kuirau Park Geothermal Walk
- **Distance & Duration**: 2.0 km loop | 45 minutes
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Easy (paved walkways and footbridges)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Kuirau Park car park (Ranolf Street / Lake Road, Rotorua CBD edge).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs allowed on leash on paved paths. Free public mineral footbaths, children's playground, and modern public toilets.

Located right on the edge of Rotorua’s city centre, Kuirau Park is New Zealand’s only free public geothermal reserve. Well-fenced boardwalks guide you safely past violently bubbling mud pools, steaming turquoise sulfuric lakes, and hiss-vents erupting through the lawn. The park features free, covered thermal footbaths where you can sit and soak your feet in mineral-rich geothermal water after a morning of hiking.

> **Local Trail Tip**: **Strict Safety Warning**: Never step off the marked boardwalks or climb over wooden barriers. Geothermal crust can be paper-thin and hides boiling mud and water reaching 100°C. Visit on a Saturday morning to browse the lively Kuirau Park Rotary Farmers Market.

### 7. Western Okataina Walkway (Lake Okataina to Ruato Bay)
- **Distance & Duration**: 11.0 km one-way (or 4.0 km return to Trig lookout) | 3–4 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +280 m | Moderate (remote backcountry bush trail)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Lake Okataina main beach car park (end of Lake Okataina Road).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (high-value native wildlife sanctuary). DOC toilets and boat ramp at lakehead.

Lake Okataina ("the lake of laughter") is the most wild and untouched of all Rotorua's crater lakes. Hemmed in by sheer volcanic ridges and dense native jungle with zero lakeside houses, Okataina feels completely isolated from modern life. The Western Okataina Walkway follows ancient Māori portage routes through dense podocarp forest draped in supplejack vines and moss, climbing to ridge crests overlooking the jewel-like lake and the distant caldera rim.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Because of the dense forest canopy, this track stays damp and cool even during hot summer months. Carry insect repellent for sandflies near the shoreline.

## 🌋 Rotorua Geothermal & Forest Safety Rules

1. **Thermal Hazard Awareness**: In geothermal zones (Kuirau Park, Tarawera), never enter non-designated water sources. Scalding water and toxic hydrogen sulfide gas pockets can be fatal. Only soak in verified public pools or areas where hot spring flows mix with open lake water.
2. **Kauri & Myrtle Rust Biosecurity**: Clean your footwear thoroughly at trail entrances to prevent the spread of forest pathogens into native podocarp sanctuaries.
3. **Secure Your Vehicle**: Do not leave visible luggage, bags, or electronics in vehicles at remote trailhead car parks (such as Tarawera Trail or Blue Lake).

## Before You Hit the Trail: Rotorua Trail Answers

**Is the Redwoods Forest free to walk in?**
Yes. The entire Whakarewarewa Forest trail network (over 100 km of walking and mountain bike tracks) is 100% free to access from dawn to dusk. The only paid attraction is the commercial "Redwoods Treewalk" suspended bridge attraction.

**Can you swim in Rotorua's lakes?**
Yes! Lake Tikitapu (Blue Lake), Lake Tarawera, and Lake Ōkāreka are pristine and fantastic for swimming during the summer months. Always check local regional council signage for temporary seasonal algal bloom warnings before diving in.

**How do I get to the Tarawera Trail?**
The trailhead on Tarawera Road is a 15-minute drive from central Rotorua. If you plan to hike one-way to Hot Water Beach, book your return water taxi through *Totally Tarawera* online prior to setting off.
