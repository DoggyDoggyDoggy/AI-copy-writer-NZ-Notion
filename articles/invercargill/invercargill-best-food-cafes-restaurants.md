# Where to Eat in Invercargill: The Ultimate Southland Food & Kai Guide

Guarding the southernmost edge of New Zealand, Invercargill (Waihōpai) is a culinary sanctuary where food is honest, unpretentious, and deeply satisfying. This is a place where southern hospitality (*manaakitanga*) is not a slogan—it is served hot with melted butter on toasted bread.

Southland boasts some of the most celebrated and protected culinary delicacies in the world: the legendary Southland Cheese Roll (affectionately known across the country as "Southern Sushi"), wild dredge Bluff oysters harvested from the icy currents of Foveaux Strait, and pristine king salmon raised in Stewart Island's sub-Antarctic waters. Alongside our [Invercargill Travel Guide](/blog/invercargill-travel-guide) and [Invercargill Budget Guide](/blog/invercargill-on-a-budget-tips), here is your essential guide to the best cafes, bakeries, and southern kai in Invercargill.

## Best Coffee & The Legendary Cheese Roll

In Southland, coffee is almost always accompanied by a cheese roll—a regional staple made by rolling white bread around a secret mixture of sharp cheddar, evaporated milk, and onion soup mix, then grilling it until crispy and golden.

### The Batch Cafe (173 Spey Street)
Widely celebrated by locals and food critics as the gold standard of the Southland cheese roll.
- **The Experience**: Cozy vintage armchairs, sunny streetfront windows, and the aroma of freshly pulled espresso.
- **The Ritual**: Order two hot cheese rolls with generous butter alongside a silky flat white ($11–$13 NZD total). Their seasonal breakfast menu also features Southland oat porridge and salmon eggs benedict.

### The Cheeky Llama Cafe (Inside Queens Park)
Nestled right in the centre of the 80-hectare Queens Park next to the bird aviary.
- **The Setting**: Sit on the outdoor deck under mature trees watching alpacas and park birds.
- **The Treats**: Exceptional morning scones, savoury frittatas, and specialty coffee.

### Industry Cafe (33 Arena Avenue / Stadium Southland)
A bustling, high-energy cafe known for hearty breakfasts, towering pancake stacks, and fast takeaway coffee.

## Iconic Southern Kai: Bluff Oysters & Coastal Seafood

### Bluff Oysters (In Season: March to August)
Considered by global gourmets to be the finest wild oysters on earth—plump, intensely briny, and sweet.
- **Where to Eat**: If visiting between March and August, head 25 minutes south to Bluff. Visit **Oyster Cove** perched directly above Stirling Point, or buy a freshly shucked pottle directly from harbour processing sheds to eat with lemon on the wharf.
- **Off-Season**: If visiting outside the winter season, local bistros serve frozen-at-sea Bluff oysters and fresh South Island blue cod year-round.

### Level One Restaurant & Bar (Kelvin Hotel / Kelvin St)
Invercargill’s premier restaurant celebrating Southland provenance.
- **The Menu**: Savor pristine Big Glory Bay king salmon from Stewart Island, Southland salt-marsh lamb cutlets with mint jus, and Fiordland venison loin paired with Central Otago Pinot Noir.

### The Saucy Chef on St Andrew (Corner St Andrew & Sydney Streets)
A warm, welcoming local institution known for giant steaks, slow-braised pork belly, and comforting seafood chowders.

## Eating in Invercargill: A Few Honest Questions Answered

**What is Invercargill most famous for food-wise?**
The iconic **Southland Cheese Roll** and wild **Bluff Oysters**. You cannot leave Southland without eating a toasted cheese roll at *The Batch Cafe*. If you are here between March and August, fresh Bluff oysters are a mandatory culinary pilgrimage.

**Is Invercargill good for vegetarians and vegans?**
Good and accommodating. Modern cafes like *The Batch Cafe* and *The Cheeky Llama* offer delicious plant-based breakfasts, vegan slices, and dairy-free smoothies.

**What's the average cost of eating out in Invercargill?**
Invercargill is one of the cheapest cities in New Zealand for dining. Expect to pay **$14–$18 NZD** for a cafe breakfast with coffee and a cheese roll, **$12–$16 NZD** for a casual fish-and-chip lunch, and **$45–$70 NZD per person** for a sit-down dinner with wine at a premier restaurant.
