# 7 Best Hikes & Scenic Walks in New Plymouth: Taranaki Peaks, Coastal Bridges & Goblin Forests

In New Plymouth (Ngāmotu), everything exists in the magnetic gravitational pull of **Mount Taranaki**—the solitary, symmetrical 2,518-metre volcanic cone rising straight out of the dairy pastures into the clouds. The mountain commands the skyline, dictates the coastal weather, and feeds dozens of cascading rivers running west into the Tasman Sea.

The tramping variety in this western peninsula is extraordinary. You can start your morning climbing a 153-metre sheer volcanic sea stack using steel chains, walk across an award-winning coastal suspension bridge shaped like a breaking wave, and spend the afternoon hiking through the gnarled, moss-draped "Goblin Forests" of Egmont National Park to reach the famous Pouakai mirror tarns. 

Here is the local tramper’s guide to the seven best walks and hikes across New Plymouth and the Taranaki region.

### 1. Pouākai Crossing & Pouākai Tarns (Egmont National Park)
- **Distance & Duration**: 19.0 km one-way (full Crossing) or 12.0 km return to Tarns from Mangorei Road | 5–6 hours return to Tarns
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +700 m | Strenuous (steep wooden stairs, alpine boardwalks, exposed volcanic ridge)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Mangorei Road end car park (for the direct route to the Tarns via Pouākai Hut). Car park fills very early on clear weekends.
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (National Park sanctuary). DOC toilets at Pouākai Hut.

This is the most iconic alpine day walk in the North Island. Starting from the end of Mangorei Road, you'll climb a relentless staircase of timber steps through the ancient native kamahi forest before emerging onto the open alpine tussock plateau. Past the Pouākai Hut, a wooden boardwalk navigates the fragile Ahukawakawa alpine swamp to reach the famous **Pouākai Tarns**. On a calm, windless day, the still surface of the tarn reflects the majestic volcanic cone of Mount Taranaki with absolute photographic perfection.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Check wind forecasts meticulously. To capture the world-famous mirror reflection, you need zero surface wind. Start climbing by 6:00 AM; morning alpine winds are generally calmest before thermal afternoon mountain breezes kick in.

### 2. New Plymouth Coastal Walkway & Te Rewa Rewa Bridge
- **Distance & Duration**: 13.2 km one-way (customizable sections) | 2–3 hours walk (or 1 hour by bike)
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Easy (fully paved, world-class multi-use promenade)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Breakwater Bay / Port Taranaki, or Lake Rotomanu / Te Rewa Rewa Bridge car park.
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted. Restrooms, cafes, bike hire, and public art throughout.

An award-winning paved coastal promenade that traces the entire ocean frontage of New Plymouth from Port Taranaki east to Bell Block. The undisputed visual highlight is the **Te Rewa Rewa Bridge**, a 68-metre pedestrian suspension bridge whose gleaming white steel ribs evoke the shape of a breaking Tasman wave or a sacred whale skeleton. Standing at the northern portal of the bridge, the arching white beams frame the distant snowy peak of Mount Taranaki in a breathtaking alignment.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Hire an e-bike or cruiser from the kiosk at the central waterfront. Ride from Port Taranaki out past the Len Lye Wind Wand to Te Rewa Rewa Bridge, then grab a coffee at **The Fitting Room** or **Beach Street Cafe**.

### 3. Paritutu Rock Summit Scramble
- **Distance & Duration**: 1.0 km return | 30–45 minutes
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +153 m | Strenuous / Technical (near-vertical rock scramble using fixed steel chains)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Paritutu car park (Centennial Drive, near Ngamotu Beach).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs**. No facilities on rock.

Rising 153 metres sheer above the Tasman Sea, Paritutu is a rugged volcanic plug—the eroded remnant of an ancient volcanic vent that was active long before Mount Taranaki erupted. The climb starts with steep wooden stairs before transitioning into an adrenaline-pumping, near-vertical scramble up bare volcanic rock using heavy-duty steel safety chains bolted into the rock face. The small summit platform delivers a staggering 360-degree panorama: looking straight down on the Sugar Loaf Islands marine reserve, the industrial Port of Taranaki, and the sweeping sweep of the Taranaki coastline framed by Mount Taranaki.

> **Local Trail Tip**: **Do not attempt in wet or stormy weather**. The rock face becomes lethally slick when wet. Wear shoes with sticky rubber soles and leave bulky backpacks in your car.

### 4. Lake Mangamahoe Circuit & Bridle Track
- **Distance & Duration**: 6.0 km loop | 1.5–2 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +120 m | Easy to Moderate (well-formed forest and lake paths)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Lake Mangamahoe main car park (off State Highway 3, 10 mins south of New Plymouth).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted on designated tracks (strictly prohibited around lake edge wildlife). Toilets and picnic areas on site.

Lake Mangamahoe is a 262-hectare scenic reserve that provides New Plymouth’s second most famous reflection view of Mount Taranaki. The circuit track meanders through dense native bush, tranquil lake inlets, and towering groves of exotic Californian redwoods and eucalyptus. From the designated lookout hill on the eastern shore, the symmetrical cone of Taranaki is mirrored across the calm, tree-fringed reservoir waters.

> **Local Trail Tip**: This reserve is home to extensive mountain bike trails. Keep to the dedicated walking tracks and watch for equestrian riders on the outer bridle trails.

### 5. Dawson Falls & Wilkies Pools Loop (Egmont National Park)
- **Distance & Duration**: 3.5 km loop | 1.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +180 m | Easy to Moderate (well-graded paths and boardwalks)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Dawson Falls Visitor Centre car park (end of Manaia Road, Kaponga).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (National Park). Visitor centre, historic lodge, and toilets.

Located on the southern slopes of Mount Taranaki, this walk leads through the surreal **Goblin Forest**—an ancient native kamahi forest where gnarled, twisted tree trunks are completely enveloped in thick hanging mosses, liverworts, and delicate ferns. The trail leads to **Wilkies Pools**, a series of natural plunge pools and water chutes carved out of 20,000-year-old volcanic lava rock by glacial meltwater from the Kapuni Stream. A short side track descends to the base of the roaring 18-metre **Dawson Falls (Te Rere o Noke)**.

> **Local Trail Tip**: In summer, bring swimwear. Wilkies Pools feature natural rock water slides and deep, crystal-clear (and bracingly cold!) volcanic swimming holes.

### 6. Pukekura Park Nature & Fernery Trails
- **Distance & Duration**: 4.0 km (customizable pathways) | 1–1.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat to gentle | Easy (paved pathways, wooden footbridges)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Main entrance car park (Fillis Street or Liardet Street, New Plymouth).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted. Historic tea house, playgrounds, rowboat hire, and accessible restrooms.

Spanning 52 hectares right in the heart of the city, Pukekura Park is widely regarded as one of New Zealand's premier botanical parklands. Wander across the iconic red Poet’s Bridge over Lake Rotoroa, explore the illuminated subterranean **Fernery & Display Houses** showcasing rare native and exotic ferns, and follow winding bush trails beneath giant native king ferns, kauri, and flowering rhododendrons.

> **Local Trail Tip**: If visiting between mid-December and late January, return after dark for the world-famous **TSB Festival of Lights**, when the entire park transforms into a magical illuminated wonderland of light sculptures and music.

### 7. Whitecliffs Walkway (Pukearuhe to Wai-pingao Stream)
- **Distance & Duration**: 11.0 km return (or shorter 6 km section) | 3–4 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +240 m | Moderate (coastal farmland, cliff tracks, and beach walk)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: End of Pukearuhe Road, Pukearuhe (45 mins north of New Plymouth).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (private farmland and conservation covenant).

For rugged, windswept coastal drama, head north to the Whitecliffs Walkway. The trail crosses open coastal sheep pastures high above sheer, pale siltstone cliffs dropping vertically into the Tasman Sea. The track descends through coastal native forest to Wai-pingao Stream and passes through a historic 1880s gas pipeline tunnel cut directly through the cliff rock.

> **Local Trail Tip**: The beach section of this walk can only be negotiated at **low tide**. Check tide charts carefully or use the clifftop farm bypass track if the tide is rising.

## ⛰️ Mount Taranaki Alpine Safety & Tramper Rules

1. **Mountain Weather Unpredictability**: Mount Taranaki is an isolated coastal volcano that creates its own microclimate. Freezing squalls, dense cloud, and sub-zero wind chills can hit within 30 minutes. Always pack waterproof outer layers, thermal clothing, and a charged phone.
2. **Alpine Boardwalk Protection**: Ahukawakawa Swamp is a globally unique alpine wetland home to rare carnivorous plants and endemic insects. Strictly stay on the raised timber boardwalks at all times.
3. **Paritutu Safety**: Never attempt to climb Paritutu Rock in wet, frosty, or high-wind conditions.

## Before You Hit the Trail: New Plymouth Trail Answers

**How long does it take to hike to the Pouakai Tarns?**
Starting from the Mangorei Road car park, the one-way climb up the wooden stairs to the Pouakai Hut takes about 2 to 2.5 hours. From the hut, it is an easy 15–20 minute boardwalk stroll to the tarns. Allow 4.5 to 5.5 hours total for the return trip including photography time.

**Can I walk to Dawson Falls without doing a long hike?**
Yes. Dawson Falls is only a 10-minute walk from the Dawson Falls Visitor Centre car park down a well-formed track with handrails.

**Are these tracks accessible in winter?**
The Coastal Walkway, Pukekura Park, Lake Mangamahoe, and Paritutu (on dry days) are accessible year-round. Alpine tracks in Egmont National Park (Pouakai Tarns, summit routes) frequently experience snow, sub-zero ice, and gale-force winds in winter (June to October) and require alpine equipment and experience.
