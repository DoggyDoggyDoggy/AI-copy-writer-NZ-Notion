# Where to Eat in Tauranga & Mount Maunganui: The Ultimate Food & Coffee Guide

Blessed with year-round sub-tropical sunshine, rich horticultural orchards of avocados and kiwifruit, and an ocean coastline teeming with snapper, tarakihi, and kingfish, Tauranga Moana is the culinary jewel of the Bay of Plenty.

Dining here is inextricably tied to the salt air and the iconic silhouette of Mauao (Mount Maunganui). You can start your morning with a meticulously poured flat white alongside surfboards propped against sun-bleached timber walls, eat wharf-fresh fish wrapped in traditional paper while listening to cargo cranes in the harbour, and spend the evening sipping locally brewed IPAs in lantern-lit industrial taprooms. Alongside our [Tauranga Travel Guide](/blog/tauranga-travel-guide) and [Tauranga Budget Guide](/blog/tauranga-on-a-budget-tips), here is your essential guide to eating and drinking across Tauranga and the Mount.

## Best Coffee & Morning Bakeries

The coffee culture around Mount Maunganui and Tauranga is deeply intertwined with morning beach walks and surf sessions.

### Eight Ounce Coffee (Maunganui Road, The Mount)
A sleek, sunny espresso sanctuary in the heart of Mount Maunganui village.
- **The Craft**: Passionate baristas pouring locally roasted beans with silk-smooth microfoam and deep caramel sweetness.
- **Morning Treat**: Grab an iced oat latte and an artisan cinnamon roll before heading across to Main Beach.

### Mount Sourdough Co. (Central Mount Maunganui)
An artisanal slow-fermentation bakery that commands queues out the door on weekend mornings.
- **The Bakes**: Crusty country sourdough loaves, flaky almond croissants, pain au chocolat, and savoury Danish pastries loaded with seasonal Bay of Plenty vegetables and goat cheese.

### Love Rosie Bakery (Tauranga CBD / Tenth Avenue)
A charming, vintage-inspired cottage cafe in central Tauranga.
- **The Specialty**: Renowned for incredible homemade sausage rolls, seasonal salad bowls, and comforting, homestyle cakes that celebrate local fruit harvests.

## Casual Lunch & Iconic Coastal Kai

Midday dining around Tauranga is all about fresh ocean harvests and relaxed outdoor seating.

### Bobby’s Fresh Fish Market (1 Dive Crescent, Tauranga Harbour)
This is not a fancy restaurant; it is a legendary New Zealand seafood institution.
- **The Experience**: Situated directly on the commercial fishing wharf. Pick your fresh fish fillets (snapper, gurnard, or tarakihi) caught by local boats that morning. It is dipped in light, crispy batter and served piping hot in butcher's paper with lemon wedges and hot chips.
- **The Vibe**: Sit on the outdoor timber wharf steps with your legs dangling toward the water, watching fishing vessels unload their catch.

### Rice Rice Baby (Maunganui Road, The Mount)
A vibrant, lantern-lit Vietnamese street-food eatery with neon signs and vintage bicycle decor.
- **Top Dishes**: Fragrant 12-hour beef brisket pho, crispy pork and prawn bánh mì baguettes, fresh summer rice paper rolls, and lemongrass chicken noodle bowls.

### Eddie & Elspeth (Central Parade, Mount Maunganui)
A stylish neighbourhood cafe and bakery hub combining modern brunch cuisine with artisanal baked goods and specialty coffee.

## Top Dinner Spots & Regional Dining

When evening arrives, the waterfronts of Tauranga’s The Strand and downtown Mount Maunganui come alive with fresh coastal dining.

### Mount Bistro (Mount Maunganui)
An award-winning, long-standing fine-dining benchmark led by chef Stephen Barry.
- **The Menu**: Celebrates regional Bay of Plenty ingredients, from pan-seared local hapuku to confit duck leg and local avocado salads, paired with a comprehensive New Zealand wine list.

### Clarence Hotel & Bistro (Willow Street, Tauranga)
Housed inside Tauranga's historic 1875 former post office and council chambers.
- **The Setting**: Italian-inspired Mediterranean bistro dining inside majestic heritage brick arches and a lush sub-tropical garden courtyard.
- **The Dishes**: Handmade agnolotti, wood-grilled local fish, and prime Canterbury beef cuts with truffle butter.

## Craft Breweries & Taprooms

The Bay of Plenty is a major hub for independent brewing, with several acclaimed craft houses.

### The Rising Tide / Mount Brewing Co. (Newton Street, The Mount)
The lively brewery taproom of Mount Brewing Co.
- **The Pours**: Over thirty rotating craft taps featuring their award-winning Mermaids Mite lager, juicy hazy IPAs, and seasonal fruit sours.
- **The Food**: Home to *Johney's Dumpling House*, serving handmade steamed and fried pork, prawn, and vegetarian dumplings alongside craft pints.

### Mata Brewery & Tasting Room (Whakatāne & BOP Region)
Pioneering independent brewing infused with indigenous ingredients like mānuka honey and regional citrus.

## Eating in Tauranga: A Few Honest Questions Answered

**What is Tauranga most famous for food-wise?**
Ultra-fresh ocean seafood (especially snapper, tarakihi, and kingfish) and world-renowned horticultural produce. The Bay of Plenty produces the vast majority of New Zealand’s avocados and kiwifruit. Eating fresh fish and chips at Bobby’s Wharf is an absolute must.

**Is Tauranga good for vegetarians and vegans?**
Yes. The beach and surf culture at Mount Maunganui has fostered a vibrant healthy eating scene. Eateries like *Rice Rice Baby*, *The Rising Tide*, and local wholefood cafes offer extensive, delicious plant-based menus.

**What's the average cost of eating out in Tauranga?**
Expect to pay **$18–$24 NZD** for a cafe brunch with specialty coffee, **$12–$16 NZD** for wharf fish and chips, and **$55–$80 NZD per person** for a sit-down dinner with local wine at a coastal bistro.
