# Where to Eat in Gisborne: The Ultimate Local Food & Wine Guide

Tucked away on the remote East Coast of the North Island, Gisborne (Tūranganui-a-Kiwa) is the Chardonnay capital of New Zealand and the first city on earth to see the morning sun. Blessed with alluvial river valley soils, high sunshine hours, and a vibrant Māori coastal heritage, dining here is deeply unhurried, generous, and connected to the land and sea.

Gisborne’s food scene reflects its surf-and-vineyard culture. You will find dark espresso poured in rustic garage roasteries near the beach, historic cellar doors where winemakers pour buttery, gold-medal Chardonnays with homemade platters, and ocean-fresh fish and chips eaten right off the paper at sunset. Alongside our [Gisborne Travel Guide](/blog/gisborne-travel-guide) and [Gisborne Budget Guide](/blog/gisborne-on-a-budget-tips), here is your insider guide to eating and drinking in Tairāwhiti.

## Best Coffee & Morning Bakeries

Start your morning early after catching the world's first sunrise at Wainui Beach.

### Raglan Roast Gisborne (Wainui Road)
Located in a laid-back, garage-style beachside outpost near the mouth of the river.
- **The Vibe**: Surfers checking wave conditions, vintage leather armchairs, and the smell of freshly roasted dark-roast coffee.
- **The Value**: Fantastic, rich flat whites and long blacks served for an unbeatable $4.00–$4.50 NZD. Grab a fresh cinnamon scroll or muffin to go.

### Far East Coffee Roastery (Inner Harbour)
A modern specialty micro-roaster located near the wharf, serving single-origin Ethiopian and Colombian beans alongside healthy breakfast bites.

### Village Roaster (Ormond Road)
A local institution in suburban Mangapapa, known for its extensive range of house-roasted beans and hearty homemade sausage rolls and quiches.

## Cellar Doors & Best Local Kai

Gisborne is internationally renowned for full-bodied, aromatic white wines—especially Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, and Gewürztraminer.

### Matawhero Wines (189 Riverpoint Road)
One of the most historic and romantic cellar doors in the region, established in the late 1960s.
- **The Setting**: Sit beneath shaded olive trees and grapevines overlooking rural vineyards.
- **The Wine & Food**: Sip gold-medal Single Vineyard Chardonnay, floral Gewürztraminer, and rich Syrah paired with generous Italian charcuterie boards, local cheeses, and sourdough bread.

### Millton Vineyards & Winery (119 Papatu Road)
Pioneers of organic and biodynamic winemaking in the Southern Hemisphere since 1984.
- **The Philosophy**: Certified biodynamic wines that capture the pure essence of the Te Arai river flats. Their *Te Arai Vineyard Chenin Blanc* and *Opou Vineyard Chardonnay* are world-renowned classics.

### London Street Fish Shop (Kaiti)
For authentic coastal kai, skip fancy sit-down restaurants and order traditional takeaway fish and chips.
- **The Catch**: Fresh East Coast tarakihi or gurnard, lightly battered and served with hot, crisp chips. Eat it on the grass at Kaiti Beach overlooking the harbour.

## Top Dinner Spots & Independent Craft Beer

### Sunshine Brewery & Taproom (28 Awapuni Road)
Established in 1989, Sunshine Brewery is one of the oldest independent craft breweries in New Zealand.
- **The Space**: A lively, modern industrial taproom and brewery located near Midway Beach.
- **The Pours**: Freshly poured *Gisborne Gold* lager, *Pipeline* Pacific Pale Ale, and seasonal hazy IPAs.
- **The Food**: Woodfired gourmet pizzas, loaded fries, and craft burgers served right by the brewing tanks.

### USSCo Bar & Bistro (60 Childers Road)
Set inside the beautifully restored, heritage-listed 1870s Union Steam Ship Company building in the central city.
- **The Food**: Seasonal East Coast bistro dining celebrating local producers. Expect dishes like line-caught hapuku with lemon caper butter, Gisborne citrus salads, and Hawke's Bay beef fillet paired with top regional wines.

## Eating in Gisborne: A Few Honest Questions Answered

**What is Gisborne most famous for food-wise?**
Chardonnay and sweet corn. Gisborne is universally celebrated as the "Chardonnay Capital of New Zealand" for its rich, peach-and-vanilla fruited barrel-fermented whites. Furthermore, the region produces incredible sweet corn, avocados, citrus, and wild coastal tarakihi.

**Is Gisborne good for vegetarians and vegans?**
Good and grounded. While Gisborne does not have dozens of dedicated vegan restaurants, local hotspots like *Sunshine Brewery*, *Raglan Roast*, and *USSCo Bistro* provide creative, high-quality plant-based pizzas, salads, and seasonal vegetable dishes.

**What's the average cost of eating out in Gisborne?**
Gisborne is very affordable compared to big cities. Expect to pay **$14–$18 NZD** for a cafe breakfast with coffee, **$10–$14 NZD** for fresh fish and chips, and **$50–$70 NZD per person** for a sit-down dinner with local wine at a premier bistro.
