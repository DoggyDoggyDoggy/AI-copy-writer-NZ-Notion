# 7 Best Hikes & Scenic Walks in Napier & Hawke’s Bay: Limestone Peaks & Coastal Bluffs

Hawke’s Bay is renowned as New Zealand’s sun-drenched wine capital, celebrated for its pastel-hued 1930s Art Deco architecture, world-class Syrah vineyards, and fertile agricultural plains. But beyond the tasting rooms and seaside promenades lies a landscape defined by dramatic geological upheaval: jagged limestone peaks, soaring coastal sandstone bluffs, hidden waterfall gullies, and thriving wetland sanctuaries.

Whether you want to climb the iconic "Sleeping Giant" of Te Mata Peak for sweeping views of Cape Kidnappers, walk along sheer coastal cliffs to visit the world’s largest mainland gannet colony, or wander beneath towering 90-year-old redwoods, Hawke’s Bay offers fantastic tramping terrain. Here is the local guide to the seven best hikes and scenic walks across Napier and the wider Hawke’s Bay region.

### 1. Te Mata Peak Summit & Giant Redwoods Loop (Havelock North)
- **Distance & Duration**: 5.5 km loop (Peak Trail / Redwoods Track) | 2–2.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +340 m | Moderate (steep limestone tracks and switchbacks)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Main Gates car park (Te Mata Peak Road, Havelock North) or Peak Summit car park.
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted. Modern visitor shelter, trail maps, and toilets at main gates.

Te Mata Peak (399 m) is the undisputed crown of Hawke’s Bay. According to Māori legend, the jagged ridgeline is the prostrate body of the giant Te Mata o Rongokako. Starting from the lower gates, the trail winds through a breathtaking grove of 90-year-old Californian redwoods (*Sequoia sempervirens*) planted in 1927, before ascending steeply along exposed limestone bluffs to the summit trig. The 360-degree panoramic view from the crest is legendary: the sprawling Heretaunga Plains, the meandering Tukituki River, Cape Kidnappers, and on clear days, the distant snowy cone of Mount Ruapehu.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Start at the lower car park and take the **Yellow Track** through the redwoods, returning via the **Blue Track**. Avoid the exposed cliff edge if you suffer from vertigo or during severe westerly wind gusts.

### 2. Cape Kidnappers (Te Kauwae-a-Māui) Coastal Beach Walk
- **Distance & Duration**: 19.0 km return | 5–6 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Strenuous (lengthy distance, soft sand, rocky tide-dependent shoreline)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Clifton Beach car park (end of Clifton Road, 20 mins south of Napier).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (active wildlife sanctuary). Toilets at Clifton and at the DOC gannet colony base.

One of the most extraordinary coastal wildlife walks in the Southern Hemisphere. The trail follows the base of sheer, towering 100-metre layered sandstone cliffs from Clifton out to the headland of Cape Kidnappers. Between October and April, the headland is home to the world's largest and most accessible mainland colony of Australasian gannets (*tākapu*), with over 20,000 birds nesting, mating, and performing spectacular plunge-dives into the ocean.

> **Local Trail Tip**: **Strict Tide Rule**: You can only walk this track during low tide. You MUST depart Clifton no earlier than 3 hours after high tide, and begin your return walk from the Cape no later than 1.5 hours after low tide. Check DOC and MetService tide charts before setting out; if you misjudge the tide, you will be trapped against the cliffs by the incoming sea.

### 3. Bluff Hill (Motu-o-Kura) Lookout & Art Deco Suburb Walk (Napier)
- **Distance & Duration**: 4.0 km loop | 1–1.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +102 m | Easy to Moderate (urban footpaths and public stairways)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Marine Parade / Napier CBD or park directly at Bluff Hill Domain lookout.
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted. Historic gun battery interpretation and public toilets at summit.

Perched directly over the bustling Port of Napier, Bluff Hill offers an unbeatable vantage point over Hawke's Bay and the Pacific horizon. Starting from the Art Deco city centre, the walking route winds up through steep, winding residential streets lined with heritage timber villas, hidden public stairways, and lush subtropical gardens. At the summit domain, look directly down on container ships and cruise liners berthing below, and gaze north toward Mahia Peninsula.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Grab a takeaway coffee from a CBD cafe before heading up the **Lightfoot Walkway** stairs from Marine Parade. The panoramic view over the ocean is especially spectacular as container cranes operate in the harbour below.

### 4. Tangoio Falls & Te Ngaru Stream Track
- **Distance & Duration**: 3.2 km return | 1 hour
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +120 m | Easy to Moderate (bush track with footbridges)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Tangoio Falls Scenic Reserve car park (State Highway 2, 25 mins north of Napier).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (scenic reserve). Picnic tables and toilets at car park.

Located off the coastal highway north of Napier, this lush reserve protects a cool, sheltered pocket of native podocarp-broadleaf forest. The track follows the bubbling Te Ngaru Stream, crossing wooden footbridges beneath a thick canopy of tawa, titoki, and giant silver ferns before reaching the viewing platform for the cascading **Tangoio Falls** and nearby **Te Rere o Hokioi Falls**.

> **Local Trail Tip**: This is the best walk in Hawke’s Bay on a blistering 30°C summer day. The dense forest canopy keeps the gorge delightfully cool and shaded.

### 5. Napier Marine Parade & Sunken Gardens Promenade
- **Distance & Duration**: 5.0 km return (customizable length) | 1–1.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Easy (fully accessible paved waterfront promenade)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Marine Parade (adjacent to Napier CBD).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs allowed on leash. Fountains, playgrounds, cycle hire, and cafes along route.

Lined with towering, iconic Norfolk Island pines planted in the late 1800s, Marine Parade is Napier's defining coastal promenade. The paved walkway stretches from the Napier Port breakwater south past the **Sunken Gardens** (land reclaimed from the 1931 earthquake), the historic Sound Shell, the Tom Parker Fountain, and the modern viewing platform jutting out over the gravel surf beach.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Do not swim at Marine Parade Beach—the gravel beach has a lethal shore-break and strong undertow. Instead, enjoy the ocean breeze, browse the outdoor sculpture walk, and watch the waves crash against the sea wall.

### 6. Maraetotara Historic Power Scheme & Waterfall Walk
- **Distance & Duration**: 2.0 km return | 45 minutes
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat to gentle | Easy
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Maraetotara Road car park (off Waimārama Road, 15 mins from Havelock North).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted.

A charming historical and nature walk hidden in a lush limestone river valley. The track follows the pristine Maraetotara River through native bush to the remnants of the historic 1920s Maraetotara Hydroelectric Power Scheme—one of the earliest municipal power stations in the country. The trail leads to a picturesque limestone waterfall plunging into a deep, turquoise river swimming pool.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Combine this walk with an afternoon drive down to **Waimārama Beach** for a swim at one of Hawke’s Bay’s premier golden sand surf beaches.

### 7. Pekapeka Wetlands Boardwalk
- **Distance & Duration**: 3.0 km loop | 45 minutes to 1 hour
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Easy (accessible wooden boardwalks and gravel paths)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Pekapeka Wetlands car park (State Highway 2, 12 km south of Hastings).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (high-value native wetland bird reserve). Viewing hides and interpretation panels.

Covering 98 hectares of freshwater wetlands, Pekapeka is an ecologically vital sanctuary that was saved from drainage and restored by the community. A series of elevated wooden boardwalks navigate directly through lush reed beds, tranquil waterways, and native plantings. The reserve is a birdwatcher's paradise, providing breeding grounds for Australasian bitterns (*matuku*), New Zealand dabchicks, royal spoonbills, and pukeko.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Visit early in the morning when calm conditions produce mirror reflections of the surrounding rural hills in the tranquil wetland waters.

## ☀️ Hawke’s Bay Weather Realities & Tramping Safety

1. **Summer Heat & Hydration**: Hawke’s Bay regularly records some of the highest summer temperatures in New Zealand, with highs exceeding 32°C. Te Mata Peak and Cape Kidnappers have zero shade—always carry at least 2 litres of water and wear wide-brim sun protection.
2. **Cape Kidnappers Rockfall Hazards**: The coastal cliffs between Clifton and Cape Kidnappers are composed of fragile, eroding sandstone. Never sit, rest, or walk directly beneath the cliff overhangs.
3. **Respect Private Farmland**: Several perimeter tracks around Te Mata Peak and Cape Kidnappers border working sheep and cattle stations. Always leave gates as found.

## Before You Hit the Trail: Napier Trail Answers

**Is Te Mata Peak free to visit?**
Yes. Te Mata Peak Park is a public charitable trust park and is 100% free to access for walkers, runners, and mountain bikers every day of the year.

**How do I safely plan the Cape Kidnappers walk?**
Check the tide timetable for Napier/Clifton via MetService or the Napier i-SITE visitor centre. Only depart Clifton within the 3-hour window following high tide to ensure you reach the gannets and return before the incoming tide cuts off the base of the cliffs.

**Can I reach these trails using public transport?**
Bluff Hill and Marine Parade are directly accessible on foot from downtown Napier. For Te Mata Peak, Cape Kidnappers, Tangoio Falls, and Maraetotara, you will need a rental car, campervan, or private tour operator.
