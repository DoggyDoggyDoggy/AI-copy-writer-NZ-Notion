# 6 Best Hikes & Scenic Walks in Whangārei: Volcanic Pinnacles & Coastal Sanctuaries

Perched in New Zealand’s subtropical Northland (*Te Tai Tokerau*), Whangārei is a coastal haven where jagged 20-million-year-old volcanic rock pinnacles erupt straight out of turquoise harbour waters, ancient kauri canopies shelter kiwi birds, and scenic rivers cascade over basalt curtain waterfalls.

Whether you want to climb 1,000+ stairs through lush rainforest between the towering jagged stone spires of Mount Manaia, explore the historic Māori pā terraces of Mount Parihaka, or swim at a secluded white-sand cove on the Tutukaka Coast, Whangārei offers exceptional hiking. Here is the local tramper’s guide to the six best walks and hikes across Whangārei and Whangārei Heads.

### 1. Mount Manaia Track (Whangārei Heads)
- **Distance & Duration**: 4.0 km return | 2–2.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +390 m | Strenuous (relentless 1,100+ wooden steps, steep stair climb)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Mount Manaia Club car park (Whangārei Heads Road, Taurikura, 35 mins from Whangārei).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (active kiwi sanctuary zone). Toilets and cafe nearby at base.

The most dramatic volcanic day hike in Northland. Rising 420 metres above Whangārei Harbour, Mount Manaia is crowned by colossal, jagged dacite rock pinnacles that represent ancient Māori ancestors frozen in stone. The track climbs through lush subtropical native forest filled with nīkau palms, white rātā, and giant tree ferns. As you break through the canopy at the top, wooden cantilevered viewing platforms suspended between the rock spires offer an exhilarating, vertigo-inducing view: looking straight down over the entrance of Whangārei Harbour, Marsden Point, and the sweeping Pacific coastline of Bream Bay.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Take your time on the stairs and stay hydrated. The summit viewing platform can be buffeted by intense coastal winds—hold onto your phone and hat.

---

### 2. Bream Head (Te Whara) Scenic Reserve: Smugglers Bay Loop
- **Distance & Duration**: 5.5 km loop | 1.5–2 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +140 m | Easy to Moderate (coastal farmland, sandy beach, and headland paths)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Urquharts Bay car park (end of Whangārei Heads Road).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (high-density kiwi protection zone). Toilets at Urquharts Bay.

Located at the very tip of Whangārei Heads, this coastal loop walk combines world-class ocean scenery with WWII history. The trail crosses coastal pastures to **Smugglers Bay**—a breathtaking, sheltered crescent of powdery white sand and crystal-clear turquoise water fringed by ancient pōhutukawa trees. The trail continues around Busby Head past the historic 1942 naval radar station and gun emplacements, offering views across to the Hen and Chicken Islands.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Smugglers Bay is Northland’s ultimate swimming and picnic cove. Pack your togs and snorkel gear; the clear water around the rocky reef heads is teeming with snapper and stingrays.

### 3. Mount Parihaka Summit Tracks (Hātea River to Memorial Lookout)
- **Distance & Duration**: 3.5 km return | 1–1.5 hours
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +241 m | Moderate (well-formed bush tracks and stairs)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Mair Park car park (Rurumoki Street) or Town Basin marina.
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted on selected tracks. WWII War Memorial and lookout platform at summit.

Mount Parihaka is an ancient volcanic dome rising directly behind Whangārei city. In pre-European times, this mountain was the largest fortified Māori pā in all of New Zealand, home to over 2,000 warriors. The **Dobbie Track** and **Hātea Track** climb from picturesque Mair Park through dense native tōtara, pūriri, and tree fern gullies to the summit memorial obelisk. The lookout platform gives you a commanding view of the entire city, the winding Hātea River, and Whangārei Harbour.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Stop at the excavated pā terraces near the upper ridge to view the ancient defensive kumara storage pits and strategic earthwork mounds.

### 4. Whangārei Falls (Otuihau) to Town Basin River Walk
- **Distance & Duration**: 6.0 km one-way (or 1.0 km loop around the falls) | 2 hours one-way
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat to gentle | Easy (well-graded gravel trails and wooden footbridges)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Whangārei Falls car park (Ngunguru Road, Tikipunga) or Town Basin marina.
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs on leash permitted. Toilets, picnic areas, and cafes at both ends.

Starting at the postcard-perfect **Whangārei Falls (Otuihau)**—where the Hātea River drops 26 metres over a basalt lava lip into a tranquil forest pool—this trail follows the river downstream through lush native river valleys, Mair Park, and coastal mangrove boardwalks to arrive at the vibrant cafes and galleries of the Town Basin marina.

> **Local Trail Tip**: You don't have to walk back up: catch a local city bus or taxi from the Town Basin back to your car at Whangārei Falls.

### 5. A.H. Reed Memorial Kauri Park Canopy Walkway
- **Distance & Duration**: 1.5 km loop | 30–45 minutes
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: Flat | Easy (fully accessible elevated wooden boardwalks)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: A.H. Reed Memorial Park car park (Whareora Road, Tikipunga).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Dogs prohibited. Footwear cleaning stations at all entrances.

A serene sanctuary protecting a magnificent remnant of ancient, unlogged Northland kauri forest. An elevated wooden canopy boardwalk takes you up into the mid-story of the forest, allowing you to walk face-to-face with 500-year-old giant kauri trees, towering rimu, and pukatea. The path connects directly to the picturesque **Paranui Falls**, cascading over a bush cliff.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Use the shoe-cleaning hygiene station at the entrance. Northland’s ancient kauri trees are highly vulnerable to kauri dieback disease—stay strictly on the wooden boardwalks.

---

### 6. Matapōuri to Whale Bay Coastal Walk (Tutukaka Coast)
- **Distance & Duration**: 3.5 km return | 1 hour
- **Elevation Gain & Difficulty**: +90 m | Easy to Moderate (scenic coastal bush track over headland)
- **Trailhead & Parking**: Matapōuri Beach car park or Whale Bay car park (Tutukaka Coast Road, 35 mins from Whangārei).
- **Dog Policy & Facilities**: Strictly **No Dogs** (wildlife conservation zone).

Whale Bay is frequently cited as one of the most idyllic secluded beaches in New Zealand. The trail winds from the white-sand crescent of Matapōuri Beach over a low headland shaded by ancient pōhutukawa trees and native coastal flax, before descending into the sheltered paradise of **Whale Bay**. With its powdery white sand, lush native bush backdrop, and crystal-clear calm water, it is an absolute dream for swimming.

> **Local Trail Tip**: Pack plenty of water and a beach towel. There are zero commercial shops or facilities at Whale Bay, preserving its raw, untouched tropical atmosphere.

## 🥾 Northland Tramping Safety & Biosecurity Protocols

1. **Kiwi Protection**: Whangārei Heads is one of the most successful community-led brown kiwi recovery zones in New Zealand. Over 1,000 wild kiwi roam here. Strictly observe all **No Dog** rules on tracks (Manaia, Bream Head, Whale Bay) to protect ground-nesting birds.
2. **Kauri Dieback Disinfection**: Always scrub all mud from shoes and spray Trigene disinfectant at boot-wash stations before entering kauri forest sanctuaries (A.H. Reed Park, Parihaka).
3. **Summer Hydration**: Northland's humid subtropical climate means you will sweat heavily on steep climbs like Mount Manaia. Carry at least 1.5 litres of water.

## Before You Hit the Trail: Whangārei Trail Answers

**How difficult is the climb up Mount Manaia?**
Mount Manaia is physically demanding due to more than 1,100 continuous wooden stairs, but the track is wide, well-maintained, and has handrails. Anyone with reasonable cardio fitness can complete it by pacing themselves.

**Are there entrance fees for Whangārei Falls or A.H. Reed Park?**
No. All public parks, reserves, and DOC tracks across Whangārei and Whangārei Heads are completely free to access year-round.

**Can I swim at Whangārei Falls?**
While the pool beneath Whangārei Falls looks inviting, swimming is not recommended after heavy rain due to agricultural runoff upstream. Smugglers Bay, Whale Bay, and Matapōuri Beach offer far cleaner, pristine ocean swimming.
